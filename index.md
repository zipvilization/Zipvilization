**1 SOLUM = 1 m² of Solum**

## Territory

[Territory](/world/territories/) gives Zipvilization space and capacity.

Its fundamental spatial structure begins with the Tile:

> **1 Tile = 1,000,000 SOLUM = 1 km²**

One Tile provides capacity for one Zip.

But:

> **capacity ≠ population**

A Tile does not automatically contain a Zip merely because the capacity exists.

From Tiles, larger territorial structures can exist:

| Territory | Tiles | SOLUM | Area | Maximum Zip Capacity |
|:--|--:|--:|--:|--:|
| Farm | 8 | 8,000,000 | 8 km² | 8 |
| City | 256 | 256,000,000 | 256 km² | 256 |
| State | 8,192 | 8,192,000,000 | 8,192 km² | 8,192 |
| Kingdom | 262,144 | 262,144,000,000 | 262,144 km² | 262,144 |

The total capacity increases by ×32 between territorial levels.

But:

> **scale ≠ composition**

A City does not structurally contain 32 Farms merely because its total surface is equivalent to 32 Farm-sized areas.

The canonical composition is:

**Farm**

↓

**City = 16 Farms + 128 City-level Tiles**

↓

**State = 16 Cities + 4,096 State-level Tiles**

↓

**Kingdom = 16 States + 131,072 Kingdom-level Tiles**

Each higher level contains sixteen complete Territories of the immediately preceding level plus an equal amount of native Territory belonging to the new level.

The **Farm** remains the primary territorial, maturity and historical reference.

## Time

Territory provides capacity.

[Time](/world/time/) provides development.

Zipvilization does not use an arbitrary Human calendar as its canonical biological clock.

It uses blockchain progression.

> **1 biological cycle = 65,536 blockchain blocks**

Human hours and days can be useful approximations.

They are not the canonical clock.

A valid active Farm can generate its initial population from zero at a rate of:

> **1 Zip per biological cycle**

subject to valid Territory, capacity and historical conditions.

After eight valid cycles:

**8 cycles**

↓

**8 Zips**

↓

**mature Farm**

That is:

**524,288 blocks**

Once mature, the Farm remains a population generator when valid higher territorial capacity exists.

The canonical cumulative maturity structure is:

| Territory | Generating Farms | Cumulative Cycles | Cumulative Blocks | Maximum Zips |
|:--|--:|--:|--:|--:|
| Farm | 1 | 8 | 524,288 | 8 |
| City | 16 | 16 | 1,048,576 | 256 |
| State | 256 | 32 | 2,097,152 | 8,192 |
| Kingdom | 4,096 | 64 | 4,194,304 | 262,144 |

This creates several essential distinctions:

> **Territorial capacity ≠ population**
>
> **Population ≠ maturity**
>
> **Current state ≠ complete History**

Territory can be acquired.

A fictional past cannot.

## Bloch and Population

Population does not appear merely because Territory exists.

The Farm is the primary population-generating territorial unit.

**Farm → generation rate**

**Bloch → digital-genetic emergence mechanism**

**Time → duration**

**Territory → capacity**

**History → what validly occurred**

The relationship is:

**VALID TERRITORY**

↓

**FARM**

↓

**BLOCH + BIOLOGICAL TIME**

↓

**UNIQUE ZIP**

A valid Farm generates one Zip per biological cycle while valid unoccupied territorial capacity exists.

Higher territorial levels do not introduce independent population generators.

Cities, States and Kingdoms provide additional capacity.

Their contained Farms remain the generators.

This means the same underlying logic can scale without inventing separate population systems for every territorial level.

## Current State Is Not Complete History

A current balance cannot tell the entire story.

A Territory may have existed for a long time.

Its Farms may have generated population.

Maturity may have been reached.

Later transfers may change future capacity.

None of those later changes automatically erase what validly happened before.

Therefore:

> **CURRENT STATE ≠ COMPLETE HISTORY**

and:

> **CURRENT CAPACITY ≠ HISTORICAL MATURITY**

A purchase may increase future capacity.

It does not create retroactive Zips.

A transfer may reduce or reorganize future capacity.

It does not erase valid prior History.

> **A transfer changes future state.**
>
> **It does not rewrite History.**

# 11. Verify

Zipvilization distinguishes between different kinds of truth and development state.

These terms are not interchangeable.

### Defined

The concept or rule has been specified.

### Developed

An implementation or model exists.

### Tested

It has been subjected to testing or review.

### Live

Canonical production state exists.

### Mature

Enough real development or History exists for mature behavior or experience.

### Data-dependent

Its mature form requires sufficient real quantitative or historical state.

### Experimental

It can be explored or tested without becoming Canon.

### Open

It is intentionally unresolved.

Therefore:

> **DEFINED ≠ DEVELOPED**
>
> **DEVELOPED ≠ TESTED**
>
> **TESTED ≠ LIVE**
>
> **LIVE ≠ MATURE**
>
> **EXPERIMENTAL ≠ CANONICAL**
>
> **OPEN ≠ MISSING**

# 12. Public Architecture, Protected Implementation

Zipvilization aims to show as much of its architecture as possible.

But understanding a system is not the same as reproducing its implementation.

The public documentation can expose:

- canonical meaning,
- canonical rules,
- deterministic relationships,
- invariants,
- authority boundaries,
- system responsibilities,
- and the inputs and outputs required to audit important claims.

That does not require publishing every:

- internal algorithm,
- data structure,
- historical reconstruction method,
- indexing strategy,
- query strategy,
- cache design,
- synchronization mechanism,
- operational topology,
- private tool,
- behavioral system,
- autonomy mechanism,
- or simulation implementation.

The rule is:

> **If hiding information prevents a canonical claim from being audited, enough information must be public to audit its meaning.**

But:

> **If information primarily enables reproduction of internal implementation, it may remain internal.**

In short:

> **Show the architecture.**
>
> **Protect the implementation.**
