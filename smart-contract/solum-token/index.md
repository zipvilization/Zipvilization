# Solum Token

Solum is the blockchain asset at the foundation of Zipvilization.

Before it is land,

before it becomes Territory,

before a Holder becomes a Colonist,

before a Farm develops,

before Zips emerge,

Solum is a token governed by a Smart Contract.

This page begins there.

> **Solum is the blockchain substrate.**
>
> **Zipvilization gives that substrate territorial meaning.**

---

# The token

At contract level, Solum is a fungible blockchain asset.

Its state can be expressed through technical concepts such as:

- total supply,
- balances,
- addresses,
- transfers,
- allowances where applicable,
- Taxes,
- Pool state,
- Burn,
- transaction limits,
- wallet limits,
- and other contract-defined behavior.

These are blockchain concepts.

They should remain understandable without requiring the world metaphor.

The world interpretation comes afterward.

# Solum and Zipvilization

Solum has a canonical territorial equivalence:

> **1 Solum = 1 m²**

This relationship connects the token with the world.

At blockchain level:

> **1 Solum is one unit of the token.**

Inside Zipvilization:

> **1 Solum represents 1 square meter of territorial substrate.**

The amount does not change.

The interpretation does.

This two-layer structure is fundamental throughout the project.

| Holder | Colonist |

> **Blockchain describes the mechanism.**
>

# Token and land are not two different assets

There is no separate land token hidden behind Solum.

There is no additional territorial NFT required to make Solum meaningful.

There is no second asset that replaces Solum when Territory appears.

Solum remains Solum.

Territory is derived from the canonical interpretation of Solum state.

That means:

**Token balance**

↓

**canonical Solum interpretation**

↓

**territorial capacity**

The underlying blockchain asset remains fungible.

The world layer gives that quantity structure.

# Holder and Colonist

At blockchain level, an address that controls Solum is a Holder.

Inside Zipvilization, the relevant participant is understood as a Colonist.

These terms are related.

They are not interchangeable in every context.

**Holder**

describes blockchain ownership.

**Colonist**

describes participation inside the world.

The distinction matters because blockchain mechanics must remain technically precise.

A contract transfers Solum between addresses.

It does not need to execute a function called "move a Colonist's land" for the world interpretation to exist.

# Balances

A Solum balance records how much Solum an address controls according to blockchain state.

For example:

> **10,000 Solum**

At the world layer:

> **10,000 Solum = 10,000 m²**

But that does not automatically mean:

> **10,000 m² = mature State**

Several different dimensions must remain separate.

**Balance**

determines quantity.

**Territorial thresholds**

determine structural capacity.

**Time**

determines development.

**Zips**

express biological state.

The token provides the substrate.

It does not contain the whole civilization.

# Transfers

Solum can move between addresses according to the contract rules.

A transfer changes blockchain balances.

That operation may also interact with:

- Taxes,
- maximum transaction constraints,
- maximum wallet constraints,
- launch restrictions,
- Pool mechanics,
- and other explicitly implemented rules.

The technical sequence is simple:

**Solum leaves one valid balance**

↓

**the contract applies the relevant rules**

↓

**resulting balances change**

Inside Zipvilization, those changes can alter territorial control.

But the contract should remain describable in blockchain language first.

> **The contract transfers Solum.**
>
> **SolumWorld determines the resulting world meaning.**

# Solum is finite

Solum is not designed as an endlessly expanding territorial currency.

The canonical supply is:

> **100,000,000,000,000 Solum**

or:

> **100 trillion Solum**

Because:

> **1 Solum = 1 m²**

the original territorial substrate is:

> **100,000,000,000,000 m²**

No continuing inflationary minting is required to expand civilization.

Civilization grows through structure and interaction.

Not through the creation of additional land.

This distinction is important.

The Solum token supply establishes the substrate.

It does not create a mature world.

Part of the finite Solum substrate can remain in its defined Pool state.

# Solum and Burn

Solum can also undergo an irreversible technical transition through Burn.

At blockchain level:

> **Solum is Burned.**

Inside Zipvilization:

> **the corresponding land becomes Permanent Nature.**

Again, the blockchain mechanism remains explicit.

We do not rename Burn technically.

We interpret its consequence inside the world.

This creates one of the defining inversions of Zipvilization:

**Pool**

technically holds Solum,

but represents barren land waiting for civilization.

**Burn**

technically removes Solum permanently,

but represents land whose natural state becomes permanent.

> **Dormant Land waits for life.**
>
> **Permanent Nature cannot be colonized again.**

# Solum and Taxes

Solum can also move through Tax mechanisms.

Tax does not create new Solum.

It redirects existing Solum according to the contract rules.

This distinction preserves finite accounting.

A Tax may alter:

- balances,
- destinations,
- accumulated Solum,
- and future economic state.

But it does not need to expand supply.

Inside Zipvilization, these movements can later become part of a larger economic architecture.

The contract executes the flow.

Civilization gives the flow context.

→ **[Explore Taxes](/smart-contract/taxes/)**

# Solum and Fair Access

Finite supply creates another problem.

Concentration.

If Solum can be accumulated without meaningful constraints during sensitive phases, a small number of actors may capture a disproportionate part of the finite world before broad participation can develop.

The contract therefore includes technical mechanisms intended to constrain access and concentration where canonically defined.

These can include:

- maximum transaction rules,
- maximum wallet rules,
- launch-phase restrictions,
- dynamic limits,
- and other explicit contract conditions.

Those mechanisms do not change what Solum is.

They change how Solum can move or accumulate under defined conditions.

→ **[Explore Fair Access](/smart-contract/fair-access/)**

Solum creates capacity.

Solum is a crypto asset.

It can have a market.

It can have a price.

But Zipvilization does not define Solum solely through expected financial return.

# Solum and Security

A token only has meaning if its technical behavior can be trusted to follow its contract.

Security therefore matters at the foundation.

Important questions include:

- who has privileged authority,
- which parameters can change,
- which rules are immutable,
- whether supply can be expanded,
- what permissions exist,
- how launch restrictions operate,
- how limits are enforced,
- how external dependencies behave,
- and what risks remain.

Security should describe the actual architecture.

Not make generic claims that the contract is "safe."

# ERC-20 compatibility

Solum is designed within an EVM-compatible blockchain environment and follows the ERC-20 token model where defined by the canonical implementation.

That gives the asset familiar blockchain behavior and interoperability.

The important point is architectural:

Zipvilization does not need to invent a completely new ownership primitive merely to create territorial meaning.

It can begin with a standard fungible token architecture.

Then build deterministic world interpretation above it.

> **Standard token underneath.**
>
> **Unusual world above it.**

The blockchain network provides infrastructure.

It is not the identity of Solum.

The canonical implementation can target an EVM-compatible environment appropriate to the technical requirements of Zipvilization.
