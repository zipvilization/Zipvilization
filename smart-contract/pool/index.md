# Pool

The Pool is a blockchain mechanism.

Inside Zipvilization, it is something else.

> **Dormant Land.**

Land already exists.

Solum already exists.

The finite world already exists.

But this part of the world has not yet entered active civilization.

It is barren.

Inert.

Waiting.

That distinction is fundamental.

> **Blockchain: Pool**
>
> **Zipvilization: Dormant Land**

The technical mechanism comes first.

The world interpretation follows.

---

# First: the blockchain

At contract level, the Pool concerns Solum held under a defined technical state.

The relevant questions are technical:

- How much Solum is held by the Pool?
- What address or contract controls that state?
- How does Solum enter the Pool?
- How can Solum leave the Pool?
- What conditions govern that movement?
- What limits apply?
- What events expose Pool activity?
- What permissions exist?
- Can the Pool receive Solum again?
- How does Pool state affect Supply accounting?

Those answers belong to the contract specification and implementation.

The Pool should be understandable without requiring the world metaphor.

→ **[Open the Repository](/repository/)**

# The Pool does not create Solum

Solum held by the Pool is not future minting.

It is not an inflation mechanism.

It is not territory that will be created later.

The Solum already belongs to the finite supply.

The Pool changes state and distribution.

It does not expand the world.

This distinction is essential.

> **Pool-held Solum already exists.**

Therefore:

> **Dormant Land already exists.**

The world is not expanding when Solum leaves the Pool.

A part of the existing world is changing condition.

→ **[Explore Supply](/smart-contract/supply/)**

# Existing, but dormant

This is where the technical mechanism becomes part of Zipvilization.

Pool-held Solum represents land that exists within the finite world but has not yet entered active colonization.

We call it:

> **Dormant Land**

Dormant does not mean nonexistent.

It means:

- uncolonized,
- inactive,
- barren,
- without developed territorial structure,
- without active biological development,
- and still containing future possibility.

The land is there.

Civilization is not.

---

# The world begins barren

This gives Zipvilization an initial geographical condition.

The world does not begin as a completed civilization.

It does not begin covered with Cities.

It does not begin populated by Zips.

It does not begin with States, Kingdoms, roads, industry, or political borders.

Much of the finite substrate can initially remain in the Pool.

Inside Zipvilization:

much of the planet remains **Dormant Land**.

A barren world.

Waiting.

This is important because colonization now has something real to transform.

---

# Pool is potential

Dormant Land contains future possibility.

Unlike Permanent Nature, it can still become part of active civilization where the technical and canonical conditions allow it.

This creates a simple state relationship:

**Pool-held Solum**

↓

**Dormant Land**

↓

**valid transition out of the Pool**

↓

**active Solum position**

↓

**Holder**

↓

**Colonist interpretation**

↓

**Territorial possibility**

The Pool therefore represents potential.

Not completion.

---

# Pool is not Burn

Pool and Burn must never be confused.

Both can involve Solum outside ordinary active Holder control.

But their meanings are fundamentally different.

## Pool

Solum still exists in a state that preserves future colonization potential.

Inside Zipvilization:

> **Dormant Land**

## Burn

Solum becomes permanently unavailable according to the Burn mechanism.

Inside Zipvilization:

> **Permanent Nature**

Therefore:

> **Pool = future possibility**
>
> **Burn = permanent removal from colonization**

This distinction is one of the most important in the entire architecture.

→ **[Explore Burn](/smart-contract/burn/)**

# Dormant Land is not Permanent Nature

From a superficial perspective, both may appear to be land without a Colonist.

That is not enough.

Their future is completely different.

| Blockchain state | Zipvilization | Future colonization |
|:-----------------|:--------------|:--------------------|
| Pool | Dormant Land | Possible |
| Burn | Permanent Nature | Impossible |

A renderer must distinguish them.

SolumTools must distinguish them.

Metrics must distinguish them.

Artificial Intelligence must distinguish them.

SolumWorld must never collapse them into a generic category such as:

> unowned land

because doing so would destroy their canonical difference.

---

# The reversal

This distinction creates one of the defining inversions of Zipvilization.

In conventional crypto language, a Pool is often associated with liquidity and economic activity.

Burn is associated with destruction.

Zipvilization interprets their world consequences differently.

**Pool**

contains land that is still barren.

Waiting.

Without active civilization.

**Burn**

creates land whose natural condition becomes permanent.

The technical terms remain unchanged.

We do not rename blockchain reality.

Instead, Zipvilization asks:

> **What does that technical state mean inside the world?**

The result is deliberately inverted:

> **Pool waits for life.**
>
> **Burn gives life permanence.**

---

# The Pool is not civilization

Solum existing in the Pool does not create:

- a Colonist,
- a Farm,
- a City,
- a State,
- a Kingdom,
- Zips,
- biological development,
- or maturity.

The substrate exists.

The civilizational state does not.

This separation is important because otherwise the finite Supply itself would already constitute a completed world.

It does not.

> **Solum can exist before civilization exists.**

---

# Leaving the Pool changes the world

When Solum validly leaves the Pool and enters active participation, something technically simple can have a major world consequence.

At blockchain level:

> Solum changes state or control according to the contract.

Inside Zipvilization:

> a portion of Dormant Land can begin its transition toward active civilization.

That may eventually lead to:

**Holder**

↓

**Colonist**

↓

**Territory**

↓

**Bloch**

↓

**Zips**

↓

**Time**

↓

**Maturity**

The Pool does not perform all of those functions.

It creates the boundary before them.

---

# Holder and Colonist begin after the transition

The Pool itself is not a Colonist.

An address or mechanism holding Pool Solum for technical purposes should not automatically be interpreted as a participant in the civilization.

This distinction is important for both Metrics and Artificial Intelligence.

A token balance alone may not always imply:

> active Colonist-controlled territory.

Context matters.

Pool state must remain identifiable.

Otherwise the system could falsely count Dormant Land as colonized territory simply because blockchain balances technically exist somewhere.

> **Technical custody is not automatically civilizational participation.**

# Pool and Territory

Dormant Solum does not automatically form active territorial structures.

A City requires its canonical condition.

A State requires its canonical condition.

A Kingdom requires its canonical condition.

The Pool therefore precedes active territorial classification.

Conceptually:

**Dormant Land**

↓

**active participation**

↓

**territorial thresholds**

**Farm / City / State / Kingdom**

# Pool and biological time

Time also does not need to create biological history for Dormant Land.

Blocks may continue to pass globally.

But barren land waiting inside the Pool should not acquire fictional territorial maturity simply because the blockchain itself becomes older.

Biological development belongs to valid activated structures under the canonical rules.

This protects an important principle:

> **Unused time is not stored maturity.**

If land enters active civilization later, it does not automatically receive a past it never experienced as an active Territory.

→ **[Understand Time](/world/time/)**

---

# The Pool cannot buy a past

This becomes especially important if Solum remains dormant for years.

Suppose a portion of Pool-held Solum enters active colonization much later.

The world itself may be old.

Existing Cities may be mature.

States may already exist.

Kingdoms may have history.

But newly activated land is still newly activated land.

Its location in an old world does not automatically make its own developmental history old.

> **The world may have a past.**
>
> **New territory does not inherit a fictional one.**

This keeps time meaningful.

---

# Pool and Zips

Dormant Land does not automatically generate Zips.

Zips belong to the biological architecture of active territorial development.

Therefore:

**Pool-held Solum**

does not equal

**Zip population.**

And:

**time spent dormant**

does not automatically equal

**biological development.**

This keeps the roles clear:

> **Solum provides substrate.**
>
> **Activation provides possibility.**
>
> **Bloch and Time provide development.**
>
> **Zips provide population.**

# The Pool decreases as colonization advances

If Solum leaves the Pool through valid mechanisms, the amount of Dormant Land can decrease.

This creates one of the clearest global measurements in Zipvilization.

At one moment:

> most of the world may be Dormant Land.

Later:

> a larger proportion may have entered active civilization.

That transformation can be measured.

And rendered.

The Pool therefore gives us a direct technical signal for one of the largest visual changes in the entire experiment.

> **How much of the barren world is still waiting?**

→ **[Explore Metrics](/metrics/)**

# A finite Pool

The Pool exists inside a finite Solum system.

That means Dormant Land is not infinite.

Every valid transition out of the Pool changes the remaining amount.

This gives early and late participation different world contexts.

An early Colonist may enter when almost the entire planet is barren.

A later Colonist may enter a world containing:

- mature Cities,
- States,
- Kingdoms,
- Permanent Nature,
- economic systems,
- and much less Dormant Land.

The same act of entering the world can therefore occur under radically different historical conditions.

That is part of civilization.

---

# Dormant Land can become scarce

At Genesis, barren territory may appear abundant.

Over time, it may not remain so.

As Solum leaves the Pool:

**Dormant Land decreases.**

As Solum is Burned:

**Permanent Nature increases.**

As Colonists control more Solum:

**active civilization expands.**

These processes operate against the same finite substrate.

Eventually, the question may change from:

> Where will civilization begin?

to:

> **How much unclaimed possibility remains?**

That transition can only be meaningful because the world is finite.

# Pool and Fair Access

The movement of Solum out of a limited Pool can create a concentration problem.

If a small number of participants can acquire very large amounts rapidly, they may capture a disproportionate part of the finite dormant world before broader participation develops.

This is where Fair Access becomes relevant.

Contract mechanisms may constrain:

- transaction size,
- wallet accumulation,
- launch behavior,
- or other defined forms of concentration.

Pool mechanics define availability.

Fair Access can constrain acquisition.

They are related.

They are not the same mechanism.

The beginning of public colonization has special historical significance.

Before Token Launch, eligible early supporters may exist.

But the world has not yet entered public colonization.

When the relevant launch conditions become active, Founding Colonists may receive their defined preferential opportunity to participate.

Technically:

contract conditions govern access.

Inside Zipvilization:

the first recognized participants receive an opportunity to enter a world that is still overwhelmingly dormant.

Their historical importance comes partly from that context.

They arrive when almost everything is still waiting.

# Pool and Taxes

Pool and Tax are separate contract mechanisms.

Tax moves or redirects Solum under qualifying conditions.

The Pool represents a particular Solum state.

If Taxed Solum interacts with the Pool under the actual contract architecture, that relationship must be explicitly documented.

It must not be assumed.

This preserves accurate accounting.

For every movement we should be able to answer:

- Where did the Solum originate?
- Why did it move?
- Where did it arrive?
- Is it now Pool-held?
- Holder-controlled?
- Burned?
- Or in another explicitly defined state?

- distribution,

- participation,
- concentration,

- launch dynamics,
- and the evolution of the finite world.

Tokenomics therefore needs to understand not only how much Solum exists.
