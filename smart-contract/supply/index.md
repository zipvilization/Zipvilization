# Supply

Solum is finite.

At the Smart Contract level, that statement has a precise meaning.

> **Total Supply: 100,000,000,000,000 Solum**
>
> **100 trillion Solum**

The canonical territorial equivalence is:

> **1 Solum = 1 m²**

Therefore the complete supply represents:

> **100,000,000,000,000 m²**

This is the maximum territorial substrate established for Zipvilization.

No metaphor is required to understand the blockchain rule.

There is a finite quantity of Solum.

Everything else begins from that constraint.

---

# The number

Written in full:

**100,000,000,000,000 Solum**

Scientific notation:

**1 × 10¹⁴ Solum**

Territorial interpretation:

**100,000,000,000,000 m²**

Because:

> **1 Solum = 1 m²**

The relationship is direct.

Solum is divisible as a blockchain token where the contract implementation allows it, but the canonical territorial unit used by Zipvilization is the square meter.

# Fixed supply

The total supply is fixed.

The canonical design does not depend on continuing issuance to expand the world.

There is no recurring inflationary mechanism intended to create additional Solum over time.

There is no biological emission of Solum.

Zips do not mint Solum.

Territorial maturity does not mint Solum.

Cities do not mint Solum.

States do not mint Solum.

Kingdoms do not mint Solum.

Chapters do not automatically expand Solum supply.

The world begins with a finite substrate.

> **Development can create complexity.**
>
> **It does not create more land.**

---

# No inflationary minting

This distinction matters technically.

Many token systems use issuance as part of:

- rewards,
- staking,
- emissions,
- incentives,
- mining,
- farming,
- or ecosystem growth.

Solum does not need territorial expansion to reward development.

The world develops through changes in state, not through continuous creation of new land.

Therefore:

**more participation**

does not require

**more Solum.**

**more Zips**

does not require

**more Solum.**

**more civilization**

does not require

**more Solum.**

The finite substrate remains the constraint beneath increasing complexity.

---

# Total Supply is not Circulating Supply

These concepts must not be confused.

**Total Supply**

describes the amount of Solum that exists under the relevant supply definition.

**Circulating Supply**

describes the amount considered to be circulating under the applicable technical definition.

Those values do not necessarily need to be identical.

Solum may exist in different technical states.

For example:

- held by participants,
- held by the Pool,
- transferred,
- accumulated through defined mechanisms,
- or permanently removed through Burn.

Therefore an AI or interface should never assume:

> **Total Supply = active colonized territory**

That would be incorrect.

Supply tells us how much substrate exists.

State tells us what is happening to it.

# Supply and the Pool

A significant distinction exists between Solum that forms part of the finite supply and Solum that has entered active distribution.

Solum held in the relevant Pool remains part of the technical architecture of supply according to the contract's actual accounting.

But inside Zipvilization, its state is different.

It is interpreted as:

> **Dormant Land.**

The land exists.

Its potential exists.

But it has not yet entered active colonization through the relevant mechanism.

This gives us:

**Blockchain**

Pool-held Solum

**Zipvilization**

Dormant Land

The Pool does not create additional land.

It contains part of the finite substrate.

# Dormant does not mean nonexistent

This distinction is fundamental.

Dormant Land is not territory outside the supply.

It is not hypothetical future issuance.

It is not Solum waiting to be minted.

It already belongs to the finite structure of the world.

Its state is dormant.

That means the world can begin with an enormous amount of existing territory that has not yet been colonized.

Technically:

> the Solum already exists within the relevant supply architecture.

Inside Zipvilization:

> the land exists, but civilization has not reached it.

This is why Dormant Land can recede without total world size increasing.

# Supply and Holders

When Solum is controlled by an address, the blockchain sees a balance.

For example:

> **Address balance: 10,000 Solum**

At the world layer, the canonical equivalence gives that position a territorial scale:

> **10,000 Solum = 10,000 m²**

The blockchain does not need to call the Holder a Colonist.

It only needs to preserve the balance correctly.

Zipvilization interprets the valid state.

**Holder**

↓

**Solum balance**

↓

**controlled territorial substrate**

↓

**Colonist interpretation**

where the canonical conditions are satisfied.

# Supply and Territory are not the same thing

Solum represents territorial substrate.

That does not mean every Solum unit independently constitutes a mature Territory.

Territorial structures follow their own canonical thresholds.

For example:

| Territory | Solum scale |
|:----------|------------:|
| Farm | 8 |
| City | 256 |
| State | 8,192 |
| Kingdom | 262,144 |

Supply answers:

> **How much Solum exists?**

Territorial rules answer:

> **What structures can a given Solum position support?**

Biological rules answer:

> **How developed are those structures?**

These questions must remain separate.

# A finite substrate supports variable civilization

The amount of land is fixed.

The civilization is not.

This is one of the most important consequences of Supply.

The same finite substrate can support radically different world states.

At one moment:

- most Solum may remain dormant,
- few Colonists may exist,
- Farms may dominate,
- and biological development may be young.

Later:

- more Solum may be controlled,
- Cities may have matured,
- States may exist,
- Zips may number in the millions,
- Permanent Nature may have expanded,
- and complex economic systems may be active.

The substrate did not need to grow.

The state became more complex.

> **Finite land.**
>
> **Unbounded historical complexity.**

# Burn changes available supply

Burn introduces a different kind of state transition.

When Solum is Burned according to the contract mechanism, that Solum becomes permanently unavailable under the corresponding technical rules.

This is not Dormant Land.

Dormant Solum can potentially enter active colonization.

Burned Solum cannot.

That creates an essential distinction:

**Dormant**

exists and may become active.

**Burned**

is permanently unavailable.

Inside Zipvilization:

**Dormant Land**

may still become civilization.

**Permanent Nature**

cannot return to colonization.

→ **[Explore Burn](/smart-contract/burn/)**

---

# Burn does not create new Solum elsewhere

The consequence of Burn should remain simple.

If Solum becomes permanently unavailable, the system does not compensate by minting replacement Solum elsewhere.

Otherwise Burn would not reduce colonizable scarcity.

The sequence is:

**Initial finite substrate**

↓

**Some Solum is Burned**

↓

**Less Solum remains available for future control**

There is no replacement mint that restores the previous amount of available territory.

Permanent removal must have a permanent consequence.

That is what gives the operation meaning.

---

# Total world and colonizable world can diverge

This creates two useful ways to think about world scale.

The original territorial substrate is established from the canonical Solum supply.

But over time, Burn can permanently change how much of that substrate remains colonizable.

Conceptually:

> **Original World = finite Solum substrate**

while:

> **Potentially Colonizable World = finite substrate − permanently unavailable Solum**

Inside Zipvilization, the difference becomes **Permanent Nature**.

The planet does not need to disappear when Solum is Burned.

Its status changes.

That distinction is important.

> **Burn does not erase the world.**
>
> **It changes what civilization can ever claim again.**

The finite substrate can be divided conceptually into states such as:

- and other explicitly defined canonical states, if applicable.

But the underlying idea is powerful:

> **The finite supply can become a measurable geography of world transformation.**

# Conservation matters

Supply accounting should remain internally coherent.

A state transition may change where Solum is held.

A Tax may move Solum.

A transfer may redistribute Solum.

The Pool may release Solum according to its rules.

Burn may permanently remove Solum from availability.

But those operations should not be confused with arbitrary creation.

For every mechanism we should be able to ask:

- Where did the Solum come from?
- Where did it go?
- Did ownership change?
- Did availability change?
- Did total supply accounting change?
- Was anything Burned?
- Was anything minted?

The answers should reconcile.

This is basic blockchain accounting.

It is also essential for world integrity.

# Taxes move Solum

Tax and Supply are related but distinct.

A Tax can redirect or accumulate existing Solum according to the contract mechanism.

That does not automatically mean new Solum has been created.

The sequence may be understood as:

**Transfer**

↓

**Tax calculation**

↓

**portion redirected**

↓

**destination defined by contract**

The relevant Supply accounting should follow the actual movement.

The Tax mechanism must therefore explain:

- the source,
- the amount,
- the destination,
- and the resulting balances.

→ **[Explore Taxes](/smart-contract/taxes/)**

# Fair Access does not change the world size

Transaction and wallet limits can restrict how Solum moves or accumulates.

They do not need to change the finite Supply.

A maximum transaction rule answers:

> **How much can move under these conditions?**

A maximum wallet rule answers:

> **How much can this address hold under these conditions?**

Supply answers:

> **How much Solum exists?**

These are separate contract dimensions.

That separation matters when analyzing Fair Access.

# Why 100 trillion?

The number defines the scale of the world.

It is large enough to support enormous territorial distribution while preserving the direct relationship:

> **1 Solum = 1 m²**

The important architectural property is not simply that the number sounds large.

It is that the world remains:

**finite**

and

**measurable.**

Every Solum belongs to the same finite substrate.

Every controlled square meter exists relative to that whole.

Every Burn changes that whole's future availability.

Every Pool balance represents part of the world still waiting.

This allows the entire territorial experiment to be expressed against a known maximum scale.

---

# Percentage of the world

Because Supply is finite, any Solum quantity can be expressed as a proportion of the original substrate.

For a quantity **S** of Solum:

> **World Share = S / 100,000,000,000,000**

As a percentage:

> **World Share % = (S / 100,000,000,000,000) × 100**

This allows the same framework to describe:

- Holder concentration,
- Dormant Land,
- Permanent Nature,
- active distribution,
- or other valid supply states.

The denominator is known.

That makes global proportions meaningful.

---

# Example: 10,000 Solum

Suppose an address holds:

> **10,000 Solum**

Its share of the original finite supply is:

> **10,000 / 100,000,000,000,000 = 0.0000000001**

As a percentage:

> **0.00000001%**

Its territorial interpretation is:

> **10,000 m²**

These are three descriptions of the same quantity:

**Token**

10,000 Solum

**Global supply**

0.00000001%

**Zipvilization**

10,000 m² of territorial substrate

The quantity has not changed.

Only the perspective has.

# Supply creates scarcity without defining value

A finite supply creates scarcity.

It does not mathematically guarantee market value.

This distinction should remain explicit.

Price depends on market behavior.

Demand may rise.

Demand may fall.

Liquidity may vary.

Participation may succeed or fail.

Finite supply does not promise appreciation.

What Supply guarantees under the canonical technical model is much narrower:

> **the territorial substrate is not expanded through ongoing inflationary creation.**

That is a structural property.

Not an investment promise.

Inside Zipvilization, scarcity becomes more interesting than token scarcity alone.

A finite world means:

- Colonists share a bounded substrate,
- Dormant Land cannot remain infinite,
- Permanent Nature reduces future colonizable availability,
- territorial concentration becomes measurable,
- distribution matters,
- and historical decisions can constrain future civilization.

Scarcity therefore participates in world formation.

It does not merely support a market narrative.

# Supply and time

Supply itself does not mature.

Solum does not become more Solum because blocks pass.

A balance held for longer does not automatically increase token quantity.

Time belongs to biological development.

This gives us another important separation:

> **Solum quantity ≠ Territorial maturity**

A Colonist can acquire enough Solum to support a higher territorial scale.

That does not mean the corresponding biological structure instantly possesses its mature state.

> **Land can be acquired.**
>
> **History cannot.**

# Supply and Zips

Zips also do not increase Solum supply.

They are population.

Not land.

The relationship must remain:

> **Solum = territorial substrate**

while:

> **Zips = native population**

More population can emerge within the finite territorial architecture without creating additional Solum.

This distinction allows biological complexity to grow while the world remains bounded.

**Solum amount**
