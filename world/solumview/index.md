# SolumView

A world can be observed from above.

But eventually, we want to enter it.

**SolumView is where Territory becomes an experience.**

SolumTools makes the state of Solum readable.

SolumWorld makes that state visible.

SolumView begins when broad observation is no longer enough and we enter the Territory of a specific Colonist.

From planet to Territory.

From Territory to internal structure.

From structure to Farms.

From Farms to population.

From population to construction, movement, development and History.

And eventually, toward a world that can increasingly feel alive.

> **SolumTools can begin with data.**
>
> **SolumWorld can begin with a planet.**
>
> **SolumView needs a living world.**

→ **[Explore SolumWorld](/world/solumworld/)**  
→ **[Discover Colonists](/world/colonists/)**

# The Basic Model

SolumView belongs to the same underlying architecture as SolumTools and SolumWorld.

The direction of authority remains:

**BLOCKCHAIN STATE + HISTORY**  
↓  
**CANONICAL RULES**  
↓  
**DETERMINISTIC ZIPVILIZATION STATE**  
↓  
**REPRESENTATION / EXPERIENCE**

Within the dApp:

**SOLUMTOOLS**  
↓  
**DATA / READ**

**SOLUMWORLD**  
↓  
**WORLD / SEE**

**SOLUMVIEW**  
↓  
**LIFE / ENTER**

These are different depths of one system.

Not different realities.

And not necessarily a rigid development chronology.

> **The architecture is modular.**
>
> **The experience is unified.**
>
> **One dApp. One world. Increasing depth.**

# A Different Kind of Layer

SolumView is not simply SolumWorld with more zoom.

The transition is qualitative.

SolumWorld asks:

> **What does Solum look like?**

SolumView asks:

> **What is happening inside this Territory?**

That difference changes the requirements.

A planetary representation can begin with very few visual states.

A territorial experience cannot.

To enter a Territory meaningfully, there must be enough underlying reality to make that experience meaningful.

Population matters.

Maturity matters.

History matters.

Territorial structure matters.

Activity matters.

Relationships between those elements matter.

SolumView therefore begins from a stronger dependency on real accumulated state.

> **SolumWorld represents a world.**
>
> **SolumView must make that world feel inhabited.**

# Territory Is More Than Balance

A current SOLUM balance can indicate current territorial capacity.

It cannot explain the complete state of a Territory.

A meaningful SolumView may eventually need to understand:

- current SOLUM,
- historical SOLUM state,
- territorial capacity,
- Farms,
- Bloch,
- valid biological cycles,
- Zip population,
- maturity,
- previous development,
- state transitions,
- and accumulated History.

Therefore:

> **CURRENT BALANCE ≠ COMPLETE TERRITORY**

And:

> **CURRENT CAPACITY ≠ HISTORICAL MATURITY**

A Colonist who acquires more SOLUM today does not receive retroactive biological development.

A later transfer does not erase development that validly occurred in the past.

SolumView must inherit the same historical integrity as SolumTools and SolumWorld.

> **A transfer changes future conditions.**
>
> **It does not rewrite History.**

# Territory Has Internal Structure

Territory is not a single undifferentiated surface.

Canonical territorial levels are:

| Territory | Tiles | SOLUM | Area | Maximum Zip Capacity |
|:----------|------:|------:|-----:|---------------------:|
| Farm | 8 | 8,000,000 | 8 km² | 8 |
| City | 256 | 256,000,000 | 256 km² | 256 |
| State | 8,192 | 8,192,000,000 | 8,192 km² | 8,192 |
| Kingdom | 262,144 | 262,144,000,000 | 262,144 km² | 262,144 |

But mathematical scale is not the same as structural composition.

A City contains:

**16 complete Farms = 128 Tiles**  
+  
**128 Tiles of City Territory**

A State contains:

**16 complete Cities = 4,096 Tiles**  
+  
**4,096 Tiles of State Territory**

A Kingdom contains:

**16 complete States = 131,072 Tiles**  
+  
**131,072 Tiles of Kingdom Territory**

SolumView may eventually make those internal relationships experienceable.

It must not replace them with a simpler but false hierarchy.

> **Graphical organization may simplify navigation.**
>
> **It may not redefine Territory.**

# Farms Are the Foundational Close-Up

The Farm is especially important inside SolumView.

It is the minimum active territorial structure.

It is also the primary origin of population.

Canonical relationship:

> **1 Farm = 1 Bloch**

Therefore:

**FARM**  
↓  
**BLOCH**  
↓  
**BIOLOGICAL TIME**  
↓  
**ZIP**

One Farm contains:

- 8 Tiles,
- 8 km²,
- capacity for 8 Zips,
- and one Bloch.

Its Bloch progressively generates population according to valid Time, capacity and History.

This makes the Farm much more than a graphical subdivision.

It is where Territory and biological development first connect.

> **Territory provides capacity.**
>
> **Farm provides the population origin.**
>
> **Bloch provides the generation mechanism.**
>
> **Time provides progression.**
>
> **History records what actually occurred.**

---

# Higher Territory Does Not Create New Generators

Cities, States and Kingdoms provide additional territorial and population capacity.

They do not introduce independent population generators.

Population continues to originate through Farms.

That gives SolumView a coherent biological structure.

A City grows because its Farms generate population and that population can progressively occupy the additional capacity of City Territory.

The same principle continues upward.

Conceptually:

**FARMS**  
↓  
**POPULATION**  
↓  
**EXPANSION INTO AVAILABLE TERRITORY**  
↓  
**HABITATION**  
↓  
**DEVELOPMENT**

This resembles a natural territorial process more than a sequence of arbitrary game unlocks.

Population originates.

Population expands.

Territory becomes inhabited.

Construction follows development.

The world becomes increasingly complex.

---

# Maturity Becomes Visible Up Close

Territorial capacity and territorial maturity are different.

A Colonist may possess enough capacity for a City without having a mature City.

The same applies to States and Kingdoms.

SolumView is where those differences can become particularly meaningful.

A Territory may be:

- structurally possible,
- biologically developing,
- partially populated,
- increasingly constructed,
- or mature.

Those states can produce different experiences.

But maturity must never be inferred from appearance.

The direction remains:

**VALID HISTORY**  
↓  
**MATURITY**  
↓  
**REPRESENTATION**

Never:

**REPRESENTATION**  
↓  
**ASSUMED MATURITY**

# Biological Maturity

The biological model provides a quantitative foundation for territorial development.

| Territory | Generating Farms / Bloch | Existing Population | Additional Capacity | Additional Cycles | Maximum Population |
|:----------|-------------------------:|--------------------:|--------------------:|------------------:|-------------------:|
| Farm | 1 | 0 | 8 | 8 | 8 |
| City | 16 | 128 | 128 | 8 | 256 |
| State | 256 | 4,096 | 4,096 | 16 | 8,192 |
| Kingdom | 4,096 | 131,072 | 131,072 | 32 | 262,144 |

Cumulative biological maturity:

**Farm → 8 cycles**

**City → 16 cycles**

**State → 32 cycles**

**Kingdom → 64 cycles**

With:

> **1 biological cycle = 65,536 blockchain blocks**

SolumView does not need to turn these values into visible counters everywhere.

But its experience must remain compatible with them.

A living Territory cannot mature faster simply because a graphical experience would look better that way.

# Zips Become Locally Observable

At planetary scale, population can be a number.

Inside SolumView, population can become individuals.

Zips are the native population of Zipvilization.

They are not merely decorative avatars placed over territorial statistics.

A canonical Zip must exist before SolumView can represent that Zip as part of canonical population.

This creates another important boundary:

**CANONICAL ZIP EXISTS**  
↓  
**SOLUMVIEW MAY REPRESENT IT**

Not:

**SOLUMVIEW RENDERS A ZIP**  
↓  
**THEREFORE THE ZIP EXISTS**

# Toward Live Zips

SolumView can eventually make Zips appear increasingly alive.

They may:

- move,
- occupy places,
- work,
- gather,
- travel within Territory,
- interact visually,
- participate in construction,
- express activity,
- and contribute to the perception of a living Territory.

But not every visual action needs to become a canonical blockchain event.

A Zip walking from one building to another can be part of a visual simulation.

That does not necessarily mean canonical state has recorded a migration.

A Zip visually working does not automatically establish a canonical profession.

A group gathering does not automatically establish a canonical institution.

This distinction is essential.

> **Visual life may be simulated.**
>
> **Canonical truth may not.**

# Behavior Can Be Richer Than Movement

A living Territory requires more than animated objects.

The deeper direction of Zips can eventually involve:

**CHARACTER**  
+  
**STATE**  
+  
**MOOD**  
+  
**CONTEXT**  
+  
**HISTORY**  
+  
**RELATIONSHIPS**  
+  
**INTERACTION**

That does not mean every component is already computationally defined.

It means SolumView must leave room for life to become richer without prematurely fixing unsupported mechanics.

Movement alone is not Civilization.

Animation alone is not behavior.

A profession icon alone is not identity.

A scripted interaction alone is not History.

SolumView should be capable of increasing depth while preserving the distinction between:

**what exists,**

**what is derived,**

**what is visually simulated,**

and

**what remains open.**

# Population Can Produce Construction

Territorial development has a natural relationship with population.

Conceptually:

**POPULATION**  
↓  
**HABITATION**  
↓  
**CONSTRUCTION**  
↓  
**TERRITORIAL DEVELOPMENT**  
↓  
**MATURITY**

The first Zips entering higher Territory do not need to encounter an already completed mature City.

Settlement can develop with population.

Construction can become richer.

Buildings can acquire more varied functions.

Territorial density can change.

The visible environment can increasingly reflect maturity.

This gives SolumView a powerful developmental direction without requiring arbitrary visual progression.

The environment can grow because the underlying Territory grew.

---

# Construction Is Not Yet a Complete Deterministic System

The relationship between population, construction and maturity is meaningful.

That does not mean every building type, construction sequence or architectural rule has already been canonically defined.

SolumView must preserve that boundary.

It may represent territorial development.

It may test ways of expressing construction.

It may explore how population changes the environment.

But it must not silently create canonical rules such as:

- exact building counts per Zip,
- mandatory building sequences,
- fixed professions per population threshold,
- predefined City layouts,
- automatic institutions,
- or predetermined cultural development

unless those rules are explicitly defined later.

> **Development can be represented before every visual consequence becomes a canonical mechanic.**

---

# Maturity Is Not the End

A mature Territory is not a finished Territory.

Once population capacity has been reached, Zips can still develop.

They can accumulate History.

They can become increasingly differentiated.

Creativity can emerge.

Relationships can matter.

Specialization can appear.

Examples may eventually include roles such as:

- farmers,
- builders,
- merchants,
- artists,
- scientists,
- philosophers,
- politicians,
- and other forms not yet defined.

These are examples of possible specialization.

They are not a fixed deterministic profession system.

SolumView must not allocate them artificially merely to make the Territory appear complex.

> **Population can become Civilization.**
>
> **But Civilization cannot be prewritten.**

# History at Close Range

SolumView can make History personal.

At world scale, History may appear as territorial change.

Inside a Territory, the same History can become much more detailed.

A Territory may eventually expose:

- when active development began,
- how its territorial capacity changed,
- when Farms became valid,
- how many biological cycles occurred,
- when population emerged,
- how maturity progressed,
- how transfers changed future conditions,
- and how the Territory reached its current state.

This produces a different kind of experience.

Not only:

> **What is here?**

But:

> **How did this place become what it is?**

That is one of the strongest reasons SolumView depends on real History.

---

# Current State and Historical State

SolumView must never reconstruct the past from the present alone.

If a Colonist currently has City-scale capacity, that does not prove City-scale capacity existed throughout previous Time.

If a Territory currently contains a mature population, its development must be supported by valid History.

If capacity decreases later, valid previous development does not simply disappear from the past.

Therefore:

**CURRENT STATE**  
≠  
**HISTORICAL STATE**

And:

**CURRENT BALANCE**  
≠  
**COMPLETE TERRITORIAL HISTORY**

This distinction becomes increasingly important as SolumView becomes richer.

# Zoom Must Not Change Reality

A common graphical danger is allowing level of detail to redefine state.

SolumView must avoid that.

A Farm seen from SolumWorld and the same Farm entered through SolumView are the same Farm.

A City does not gain buildings canonically because the user zoomed in.

Zips do not begin existing because the camera became close enough to render them.

History does not appear because a panel was opened.

The interface reveals.

It does not create.

> **Zoom changes resolution.**
>
> **It does not change reality.**

---

# Representation Must Follow State

The same authority rule applies inside Territory:

**STATE + HISTORY**  
↓  
**CANONICAL MEANING**  
↓  
**LOCAL REPRESENTATION**

If there are four valid Farms, SolumView cannot canonically present sixteen.

If population is 20, it cannot present 256 canonical inhabitants.

If a Territory is still developing, the interface cannot declare it mature.

If Permanent Nature exists, the local experience cannot treat it as active colonizable Territory.

If History does not support an event, the experience cannot present that event as something that actually occurred.

> **Experience may interpret truth.**
>
> **It may not manufacture it.**

SolumView can update as real state changes.

It can react to:

- territorial changes,
- valid biological progression,

# A Different Development Problem

SolumView has a fundamentally different development problem from SolumTools and SolumWorld.

SolumTools can begin with a relatively simple interface because its primary challenge is the correctness of data and derivation.

SolumWorld can begin with a relatively simple graphical representation because even a broad distinction between Dormant Land, Active Territory and Permanent Nature already communicates meaningful world state.

SolumView cannot reach its intended function through the same degree of interface simplification.

Its purpose is to make Territory feel alive.

That requires more than displaying correct values.

It requires enough real Territory, population, maturity and History to understand what a living Zipvilization Territory actually needs to express.

> **SolumView is not difficult because its interface contains more controls.**
>
> **It is difficult because its experience depends on a world that must first produce enough reality.**

# Testnet Models

SolumView can be prototyped before the live world contains enough mature data.

Testnet models are useful for that purpose.

They can help explore:

- technical architecture,
- data flows,
- rendering,
- Territory entry,
- navigation,
- scale,
- local representation,
- movement,
- graphical density,
- interface behavior,
- and possible forms of interaction.

They allow assumptions to be tested.

They allow systems to fail safely.

They allow the architecture to become more robust.

But testnet models are not the same as observing a living Zipvilization.

Synthetic or limited test conditions cannot fully reveal how a quantitatively significant real population of Colonists, Territories and Zips will behave over History.

> **A prototype can test the system.**
>
> **It cannot manufacture the evidence the system is meant to observe.**

---

# Testnet Is Not Canonical History

A test environment can produce useful technical evidence.

It does not become canonical Zipvilization History merely because the mechanics resemble the intended system.

The distinction must remain explicit:

**TESTNET STATE**

is useful for:

- experimentation,
- validation,
- development,
- simulation,
- stress testing,
- UX exploration.

But:

**CANONICAL ZIPVILIZATION STATE**

depends on the official system and its real History.

SolumView can learn from testnet.

It must not confuse testnet with the Civilization it will eventually represent.

# Data-Dependent Development

Some SolumView decisions should deliberately remain open until sufficient real data exists.

The relevant evidence may include:

- number of Colonists,
- distribution of Territory,
- number of Farms,
- emergence of Cities,
- territorial concentration,
- Zip population,
- maturity distribution,
- duration of real History,
- territorial changes,
- activity patterns,
- and the relationships between these states.

A handful of theoretical Territories cannot necessarily tell us how hundreds or thousands should be experienced.

A short test cannot necessarily tell us how years of History should be navigated.

A simulated population cannot fully tell us which patterns will matter once real population exists.

This is not an architectural weakness.

It is methodological discipline.

> **Do not design away evidence before the evidence exists.**

---

# Quantitative Scale Matters

SolumView needs more than isolated examples.

Its intended complexity depends partly on scale.

One Farm can validate a Farm representation.

It cannot reveal the full UX requirements of a world containing many Colonists, many Farms, developing Cities, different maturities and accumulated histories.

A few Zips can test movement.

They cannot reveal how a large population should be represented without becoming visually or computationally meaningless.

A single Territory can test entry.

It cannot reveal every navigation problem created by a diverse Civilization.

Therefore real quantitative scale becomes a development input.

> **More state does not merely give SolumView more content.**
>
> **It teaches SolumView what kind of experience it needs to become.**

---

# Maturity Is a Development Dependency

Population quantity alone is not enough.

Maturity also matters.

A world containing many newly activated Farms is different from a world containing:

- mature Farms,
- developing Cities,
- mature Cities,
- emerging States,
- different historical ages,
- different population densities,
- and different territorial trajectories.

SolumView should learn from those differences.

That means development depth can increase as the world itself acquires depth.

This creates a natural relationship:

**REAL STATE**  
↓  
**REAL HISTORY**  
↓  
**REAL MATURITY**  
↓  
**OBSERVED PATTERNS**  
↓  
**DEEPER SOLUMVIEW UX**

The interface does not need to guess all of that before Genesis.

---

# SolumView Cannot Be Prematurely Simplified

For SolumTools, a simple early interface can expose correct information.

For SolumWorld, a simple early planet can expose correct spatial state.

For SolumView, excessive simplification can remove the very thing the layer exists to provide:

**life inside Territory.**

A minimal Territory screen containing only balances, metrics and a static map would technically display information.

But it would not yet fulfill the deeper purpose of SolumView.

This is why SolumView should not be forced into release merely to complete a three-part interface structure.

It should appear when there is enough underlying reality to justify the experience.

> **Simple is acceptable when simplicity preserves the purpose.**
>
> **SolumView's purpose requires depth.**

---

# The World Must Teach the Interface

Zipvilization deliberately leaves part of its future open.

SolumView should respect that.

Rather than deciding in advance exactly how every mature Territory behaves, the development process can observe what the real system produces.

Which information becomes important?

Which territorial differences matter visually?

How should large populations be represented?

How should long History be navigated?

Which forms of activity deserve prominence?

How does a mature City differ experientially from a young one?

What does a Colonist actually need when entering Territory?

Some answers can be designed now.

Others should be learned.

> **The architecture provides the framework.**
>
> **The living world provides evidence for the experience.**
